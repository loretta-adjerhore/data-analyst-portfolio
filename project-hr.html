HR Workforce Analytics

Using SQL to analyse employee data, understand why people are leaving, and identify the groups most at risk of doing so.

1,000+ Employees SQL HR Analytics 2024

The Brief

NextGen Corp was experiencing a higher than expected rate of employee turnover. People were leaving across different departments and seniority levels, but the HR team did not have a clear picture of which employees were at greatest risk of leaving or what was driving the decisions to go.

The goal of this project was to use SQL to query and analyse the company's workforce data, identify the key factors linked to employee attrition, and provide insights the HR team could use to build targeted retention strategies.

What I Did

Step 01

Explored the Workforce Data

Queried the employee dataset to understand its structure — what information was available, how complete it was, and what fields would be most useful for the analysis.

Step 02

Analysed Attrition Patterns

Wrote SQL queries to break down attrition by department, job role, age group, tenure, and salary band to find where turnover was concentrated.

Step 03

Identified Risk Factors

Used aggregation and filtering to isolate the characteristics most commonly associated with employees who had already left the company.

Step 04

Built Retention Recommendations

Translated the SQL findings into practical recommendations for the HR team, focused on the highest-risk groups and the most actionable levers.

Key Findings

  • Attrition was highest among employees in their first two years Employees who were relatively new to the company were leaving at a much higher rate than those with longer tenure, suggesting onboarding and early engagement needed attention.
  • Certain departments had significantly above-average turnover A small number of teams were consistently losing staff, pointing to specific management, workload, or culture issues in those areas.
  • Salary band and overtime were strongly associated with attrition risk Employees in lower salary bands who were also working frequent overtime had the highest likelihood of leaving — a combination of being overworked and feeling undervalued.
  • Targeted retention efforts could significantly reduce turnover costs By focusing on the identified high-risk groups with specific interventions, the company could meaningfully reduce the cost of replacing departing staff.

SQL Code

Below are the key queries used in this analysis. Each one answers a specific business question about the workforce data.

query_01_attrition_by_department.sql SQL 
-- Which departments have the highest attrition rate?
SELECT
    Department,
    COUNT(*) AS total_employees,
    SUM(CASE WHEN Attrition = 'Yes' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS employees_left,
    ROUND(
        SUM(CASE WHEN Attrition = 'Yes' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) * 100.0
        / COUNT(*), 2
    ) AS attrition_rate_pct
FROM hr_employee_data
GROUP BY Department
ORDER BY attrition_rate_pct DESC;

What this does: Counts all employees in each department, then calculates what percentage of them have left. Ordering by attrition rate shows which departments have the biggest retention problem.

query_02_tenure_attrition_risk.sql SQL 
-- Are newer employees leaving more than experienced ones?
SELECT
    CASE
        WHEN YearsAtCompany BETWEEN 0 AND 2  THEN '0-2 Years'
        WHEN YearsAtCompany BETWEEN 3 AND 5  THEN '3-5 Years'
        WHEN YearsAtCompany BETWEEN 6 AND 10 THEN '6-10 Years'
        ELSE '10+ Years'
    END AS tenure_band,
    COUNT(*) AS total_employees,
    SUM(CASE WHEN Attrition = 'Yes' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS employees_left,
    ROUND(
        AVG(CASE WHEN Attrition = 'Yes' THEN 1.0 ELSE 0.0 END) * 100, 2
    ) AS attrition_rate_pct
FROM hr_employee_data
GROUP BY tenure_band
ORDER BY
    CASE tenure_band
        WHEN '0-2 Years'   THEN 1
        WHEN '3-5 Years'   THEN 2
        WHEN '6-10 Years'  THEN 3
        ELSE 4
    END;

What this does: Groups employees into tenure bands and calculates the attrition rate for each group. This reveals whether the company has a new-employee retention problem or if the issue is spread across all levels of experience.

query_03_salary_overtime_risk.sql SQL 
-- Does being paid less AND working overtime increase risk of leaving?
SELECT
    MonthlyIncome_Band,
    OverTime,
    COUNT(*) AS employee_count,
    SUM(CASE WHEN Attrition = 'Yes' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS left_company,
    ROUND(
        SUM(CASE WHEN Attrition = 'Yes' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) * 100.0
        / COUNT(*), 2
    ) AS attrition_rate_pct
FROM (
    SELECT
        *,
        CASE
            WHEN MonthlyIncome < 3000  THEN 'Low (<3K)'
            WHEN MonthlyIncome < 6000  THEN 'Mid (3K-6K)'
            WHEN MonthlyIncome < 10000 THEN 'High (6K-10K)'
            ELSE 'Senior (10K+)'
        END AS MonthlyIncome_Band
    FROM hr_employee_data
) AS banded
GROUP BY MonthlyIncome_Band, OverTime
ORDER BY attrition_rate_pct DESC;

What this does: Combines salary band and overtime status to find the combinations most strongly linked to leaving. Low-paid employees doing frequent overtime typically appear at the top of this list, confirming where retention interventions are most needed.
