GenexaHealth
Warfarin Precision Dosing Analytics

Analysing patient data to improve medication safety and identify where better dosing decisions can make the biggest difference.

50,000 Patients Power BI Healthcare 2024

The Brief

GenexaHealth is a healthcare organisation managing patients who take warfarin — a blood-thinning medication that requires very careful monitoring. Too little of the drug and patients are at risk of blood clots. Too much and they face dangerous bleeding. Getting the dose right for each patient is critical.

The organisation wanted to understand how well their current dosing was working across their patient population, identify which groups of patients were at highest risk, and find opportunities to improve outcomes through better, more personalised dosing decisions.

What I Did

Step 01

Understood the Data

Explored the patient dataset to understand what information was available — dosing history, patient characteristics, lab results, and outcomes.

Step 02

Segmented Patients

Grouped patients into four risk categories based on their dosing stability and health profile, so the analysis could be targeted and meaningful.

Step 03

Identified the Patterns

Looked at which patient groups were most likely to have dosing issues, and what lifestyle and clinical factors were driving those problems.

Step 04

Built the Dashboard

Created an interactive Power BI dashboard so clinical teams can monitor their patient population, spot risks early, and act quickly.

Key Findings

  • A significant portion of patients had unstable dosing Many patients were spending time outside their safe therapeutic range, meaning their dosing needed adjustment but had not yet been reviewed.
  • Older patients and those with multiple conditions were at highest risk These patient groups were disproportionately represented in the high-risk category, making them the priority for closer monitoring.
  • Lifestyle factors were strongly linked to dosing instability Dietary habits and medication interactions were the most common drivers of poor dosing control among the patient population.
  • Personalised dosing could significantly improve patient outcomes By targeting the identified high-risk segments with tailored interventions, the organisation could improve safety and reduce the cost of adverse events.
