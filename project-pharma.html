NovaMed Solutions | Sales Performance Analytics

1. The Business Problem

NovaMed Solutions is a leading pharmaceutical distributor serving 200+ healthcare customers across 6 countries: Canada, Australia, Germany, the UK, France, and the United States. Despite operating at significant scale — £71.31M in annual revenue across 16,314 transactions — leadership lacked the analytical visibility needed to make confident, data-driven decisions.

The business had no unified way to understand which products were driving profitability, which markets were underperforming, or how to plan for predictable seasonal demand swings. Strategic decisions about where to invest, which customers to prioritize, and how to manage a complex international portfolio were being made without data.

The core challenges: Demand forecasting was inefficient and reactive, with no visibility into seasonal patterns causing a 32% revenue swing between peak and trough months. There was no systematic way to identify which products and customers were truly driving profitability — or which were consuming disproportionate administrative resources. Without insight into customer demographics and purchasing behavior, the sales team had no foundation for targeted growth initiatives or strategic account management.

3. Analytical Methodology

01

Data Integration & Preparation

Consolidated sales data across 6 countries and multiple distribution channels. Cleaned and validated all 16,314 transaction records. Created calculated fields for profitability benchmarking, customer segmentation, and performance analysis across all dimensions.

02

Multi-Dimensional Analysis

Analyzed monthly and quarterly revenue cycles to expose seasonal patterns. Conducted product-level revenue contribution analysis across the full drug portfolio. Performed customer demographic segmentation and country-level geographic performance comparisons to map growth opportunities.

03

Power BI Dashboard Development

Built two interactive Power BI dashboards: a Sales Performance page covering revenue trends and drug analysis, and a Customer Analysis page covering demographics and buyer behavior. Interactive filtering across 6 dimensions: Year, Quarter, Country, Age Group, Gender, and Buyer Type.

04

Strategic Recommendations

Translated analytical findings into prioritized, ROI-backed business recommendations covering geographic expansion, product portfolio rationalization, seasonal demand planning, and customer engagement — each with projected financial outcomes and implementation timelines.

4. Key Insights

Insight 1 — 32% Seasonal Revenue Swing Creates a £2–3M Planning Risk

Revenue analysis revealed a significant 32% swing between peak months — June (£6.62M) and May (£6.51M) — and the annual trough in March (£4.99M). A Q4 recovery surge in November and December (£6.07M–£6.16M) confirmed predictable patterns. Despite this volatility, the 82% profit margin remained consistent throughout the year, confirming the challenge is demand planning — not underlying profitability. With the right forecasting framework, NovaMed can pre-position inventory ahead of predictable surges, protecting £2–3M in potential stockout losses annually.

Revenue and Profit by Month — 32% variance between peak (June: £6.62M) and trough (March: £4.99M) across the full financial year

Insight 2 — Classic Pareto Distribution Across the Drug Portfolio

The top 5 drugs — Doxycycline (4.95%), Ergocalciferol (4.88%), Lisinopril (4.84%), Clonazepam (4.36%), and Ezetimibe (4.25%) — generate 23.3% of total revenue combined. Meanwhile the bottom 5 performers contribute only 3.31% combined, with Warfarin the lowest at 0.32%. This confirms a classic Pareto distribution where the top 20% of drugs drive the vast majority of revenue while bottom performers consume disproportionate operational overhead — a clear case for portfolio rationalization and focused investment in proven performers.

Insight 3 — Canada Dominance Creates Strategic Concentration Risk

Canada accounts for £31.67M — a commanding 44% of total revenue — with a £15.42M lead over second-largest market Australia (£15.25M, 21%). The remaining four markets (Germany, UK, France, United States) contribute a combined 35% but show fragmented, inconsistent performance. This geographic concentration means 44% of the entire business is exposed to competitive risk in one single market, while underserved markets offer a potential 40–50% growth opportunity with focused, data-driven investment.

6. Strategic Recommendations

Recommendation 1 — Defend Canada, Accelerate Australia

Increase Canada investment by 15–20% to protect the market leader position representing 44% of total revenue. Launch an Australia expansion initiative targeting 2x revenue growth — from £15.25M to £30M — within 24 months. Consolidate European operations in Germany, UK, and France into a unified regional strategy targeting £10M+ combined revenue through focused market penetration.

Recommendation 2 — Product Portfolio Rationalization

Phase out the bottom 5 drug SKUs (£2.36M combined revenue, disproportionate admin overhead) and redirect freed resources to top performers. Double marketing investment in the top 5 drugs for a projected 25% ROI on incremental spend. Develop complementary product bundles featuring top performers to increase average transaction value and strengthen customer relationships.

Recommendation 3 — Proactive Seasonal Demand Planning

Pre-position inventory ahead of the predictable Q2 demand surge beginning in April, preventing £2–3M in annual stockout losses. Launch targeted Q1 promotional campaigns to smooth the 32% demand variance and improve resource utilization. Implement flexible staffing models aligned with seasonal patterns for a projected 15–20% reduction in operational costs during low-demand periods.

Recommendation 4 — Strategic Customer Engagement

Deepen B2B account relationships across the 88% Seller customer base through structured account management frameworks. Target the middle-aged adult demographic — the peak revenue segment generating £8.5M–£13.8M across age groups — with tailored engagement strategies. Implement a tiered loyalty programme for the top 20% of customers protecting the most critical revenue relationships from competitive attrition.
